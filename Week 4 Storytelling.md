## 1. Understand the Context

### WHO is our audience?

Our main audience is the CEO and management of Bean There Done That.

The CEO wants to know whether the company is operationally ready to grow again and what should be improved before accepting more customers.

### WHAT do we want to communicate?

Our main question is:

Is Bean There Done That operationally ready for further growth?

We want to use the company data and KPIs to:

- evaluate current performance
- identify areas that perform well
- identify operational bottlenecks
- identify problems that could become worse if the company grows
- recommend actions to prepare the company for future growth

The final conclusions will be based on the results of the data analysis.

### HOW will we communicate the story?

We will use clear visualizations supported by short explanations.

The presentation will:

1. Introduce the current situation
2. Show current operational performance
3. Present the most important KPI findings
4. Show the main bottlenecks
5. Explain what could happen if the company grows
6. Give recommendations
7. Show the expected outcome
8. Give a final conclusion about growth readiness

## 2. Big Idea

The purpose of this analysis is to determine whether Bean there Done That is operationally ready to grow next year.

The initial analysis identified several areas that require further attention before making a growth decision:

- **Inventory and waste:** Finished product scrapping totals 48,000 units, with scrapping recorded from June to August.
- **Production quality:** The two roasting machines have different rejection rates. Probat Px120 has a rejection rate of 2.54%, compared with 1.28% for Probat P60.
- **Sales data quality:** The e-commerce dataset contains 119,101 exact duplicate rows, making the reliability of the absolute sales totals uncertain.
- **Delivery performance:** Average delivery time is 1.59 days overall, but it increases to 1.91 days in December.

These findings suggest that inventory management, production quality and data reliability should be investigated before management decides how to expand.

**Working Big Idea:**

Bean there Done That may have opportunities to grow, but management should first investigate inventory waste, production quality differences and sales data reliability to make an informed decision about future expansion.

This is a preliminary conclusion based on the initial analysis. Further validation is needed before making a final recommendation about growth readiness.

### Visual Design Principles

## 5. Eliminate Clutter

The final visualizations should communicate the most important findings clearly and quickly. Unnecessary visual elements will be removed so that the audience can focus on the message.

### Design Principles

**1. Remove unnecessary elements**

Avoid decorative borders, excessive gridlines, unnecessary legends and repeated information.

**2. Use clear titles**

Each chart title should communicate the main finding rather than only describe the metric.

For example:

- Instead of "E-commerce Sales by Month", use "E-commerce Sales Increased Sharply in September".
- Instead of "Rejection Rate by Machine", use "Probat Px120 Has a Higher Recorded Rejection Rate Than Probat P60".

**3. Highlight the important information**

Use a limited number of colors and highlight only the data points that support the main message.

**4. Keep labels readable**

Use clear axis labels, consistent units and appropriate number formatting. Avoid overcrowding the charts with data labels.

**5. Use appropriate chart types**

- Line charts for changes over time.
- Bar charts for comparisons between machines or categories.
- Separate charts when measures use different units and cannot be compared clearly on the same axis.

**6. Provide context**

Include relevant time periods, units and data limitations so that the audience does not misinterpret the findings.

### Decluttering Checklist

Before including a visualization in the final presentation, check:

- Does every element help communicate the message?
- Is the main finding immediately visible?
- Are the title and axis labels clear?
- Are the colors used consistently and purposefully?
- Can any labels, gridlines or legends be removed?
- Are different units shown clearly?
- Are data limitations explained where necessary?

The goal is to make every visualization easy to understand within a few seconds and to keep the audience focused on the business message.
